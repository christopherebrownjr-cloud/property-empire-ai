# Legal verification notes (outside the P0 spec)

**Status:** background sources for the owner or counsel. Nothing here is verified. The P0 spec does not rely on any of it as law.

- **Rent cap (§10.1):** secondary sources describe a cap of 5% plus local CPI, capped at 10%. Sources disagree on the current CPI figure. Exemptions (for example, newer buildings) were not checked. Sources: [TenantCloud](https://www.tenantcloud.com/rent-collection/california-rent-increase-laws); [City of Downey](https://www.downeyca.org/our-city/departments/community-development/housing-division/ab1482).
- **Notice (§10.2):** secondary sources describe 30 days for increases of 10% or less, and 90 days above 10%. Some older law-firm pages still say 60 days. Sources: [Costa-Hawkins](https://costa-hawkins.com/ca-legislative-update/rent-increases-above-10-percent-now-require-90-days-notice-not-60/); [SF Rent Board archive](https://www.sf.gov/reports--december-2019--san-francisco-rent-board-news-archive-2019). One source states that a 5-day mailing add-on applies, and attributes it to general civil procedure rules rather than the notice statute. Unverified.
- **Deposits (§10.3):** secondary sources describe return and itemization within 21 days, with bad-faith penalties up to twice the deposit. One source describes a one-month deposit cap for leases after July 1, 2024; sources conflict. Source: [terms.law](https://terms.law/CA-Security-Deposit-Hub/).
- **Late fees (§10.3):** secondary sources describe no fixed cap, with the fee required to be a reasonable estimate of loss. Source: [Tellus](https://www.tellusapp.com/blog/late-fees-in-california/).

**Owner or counsel to verify against current official text before any figure is treated as real.**
